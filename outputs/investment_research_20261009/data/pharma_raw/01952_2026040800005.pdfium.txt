ACQUISITION OF THE ENTIRE EQUITY INTEREST IN THE TARGET

Reference is made to the March Announcement in relation to the Company entering into the
Letter of Intent with the Seller in relation to the Acquisition. The Board is pleased to
announce that, on 7 April 2026, the Purchaser, a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Company,
entered into the Share Purchase Agreement with the Seller and the Target Company,
pursuant to which (i) the Purchaser conditionally agreed to acquire, and the Seller
conditionally agreed to sell, the Sale Share; and (ii) the Purchaser agreed to take assignment
of the Shareholder Loan. The Target Company, a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Seller,
holds the asset rights, mainly including Marketing Authorization Holder (MAH) rights,
trademark and broad commercial rights for 14 branded chronic disease products across
multiple countries and regions in the Asia Pacific region. For the financial year ended 31
December 2025, the Target Company recorded a normalized revenue of US$82,232,000
(approximately RMB566,299,000) with an EBITDA of US$27,273,000 (approximately
RMB187,818,000).

Valuation Methodology
The value analysis methods generally fall into three basic categories: the asset-based
approach, the market approach, and the income approach. The following provides a brief
introduction to these three value analysis methods:
(i) The asset-based approach refers to a general term for various valuation methods that
first calculate and analyze the replacement cost of assets, then assess and quantify
various depreciation factors already affecting those assets and finally deduct these
depreciation factors from the replacement cost to arrive at the asset’s value. The
fundamental premise of the asset-based approach is to reconstruct or replace the asset
itself. This analytical technique assumes that any potential investor, when deciding
whether to invest in an asset, will not be willing to pay more than the current
replacement cost of building that asset. If the investment object is not brand new, the
price the investor is willing to pay will be the replacement cost of a brand-new asset
minus various depreciation factors. The asset-based approach is a widely used valuation
methodology.
(ii) The market approach refers to a general term for various methods that use the recent
transaction prices of similar or comparable assets in the market and, through direct
comparison or analogous analysis, calculate the value of an asset. The market approach
is an appraisal technique that, based on the principle of substitution, employs
comparative and analogical reasoning and methodologies to determine asset value. This
analytical approach holds that any rational investor, when acquiring an asset, would not
be willing to pay more than the current market price of a substitute asset with the same
intended use. The market approach is the most straightforward and persuasive valuation
method.
(iii) The income approach refers to a general term for various analytical methods that assess
asset value by analyzing the present value of an asset’s expected future earnings. The
income approach employs capitalization and discounting techniques and methodologies
to evaluate and analyze asset value. This valuation technique is based on the premise
that any rational investor, when acquiring or investing in an asset, will not be willing to
pay or invest more than the amount of money that the acquired or invested asset can
generate in the future, in other words, its expected returns. By utilizing technical tools
such as investment return analysis and discounted cash flow calculations, the income
approach takes the asset’s anticipated output capacity and profitability as the key factors
for analyzing and determining its value.
Given the characteristics of the Target Company, there are substantial limitations to both the
income approach and the cost approach in valuing the Target Company. In particular:
(i) the income approach requires subjective assumptions to which the valuation is highly
sensitive, and even small changes in key inputs (such as discount rates, growth
assumptions, or project timing) may lead to significant fluctuations in the valuation
outcome;
(ii) the cost approach is based on the replacement or reproduction cost of the underlying
assets, which does not adequately capture the value of the Target Company as going
concern; and

(iii) the Target Company derives significant value from intangible factors such as
established operations as well as future earning potential, which are not fully reflected
under the cost approach.
In view of the above, the Valuer adopted the market approach for the valuation of the Target
Company. The market approach is applied to companies for which sufficient and reliable
comparable publicly listed companies can be identified, allowing market-derived indicators
to serve as appropriate basis for estimating value.
Given the characteristics of the Target Company, EV/EBITDA was adopted as the primary
valuation multiple. EV/EBITDA is widely used in valuing pharmaceutical companies as it
reflects the operating performance of the business before the impact of capital structure,
taxation and non-cash accounting items. In addition, EBITDA serves as a proxy for the
underlying cash generating ability of the Target Company. Compared with net profit-based
multiples, EV/EBITDA is less affected by differences in financing arrangements,
depreciation policies and tax positions across comparable companies, thereby enhancing
comparability.
The Valuer selected market comparables based on the following selection criteria:
(i) companies (a) in the pharmaceutical industry under industry category of S&P Capital IQ
Tree as extracted from Capital IQ database, and (b) whose products are in relevant
therapeutic areas;
(ii) companies with their core business based in greater China and Southeast Asia
territories;
(iii) companies listed in Hong Kong, Taiwan and other Southeast Asia territories; and
(iv) companies with market capitalizations that are not significantly small in scale.
The comparable companies selected were China Medical System Holdings Limited, Grand
Pharmaceutical Group Limited, Luye Pharma Group Ltd., Simcere Pharmaceutical Group
Limited., Standard Chemical & Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Lotus Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.,
DHG Pharmaceutical Joint Stock Company and Imexpharm Corporation. Their EV/EBITDA
multiples as of the Valuation Date were approximately 12.8x, 11.5x, 6.4x, 15.6x, 7.3x,
12.2x, 10.0x and 14.6x, respectively. The adopted EV/EBITDA multiple was the median of
these multiples, being approximately 11.8x.

shareholders of the Company. C-Bridge Healthcare Fund V, L.P. (being a fund with a wide
investor base of more than 140 investors as at the date of this announcement) indirectly held
a 54.07% interest in the Seller, while C-Bridge Healthcare Fund V, L.P.’s general partner is
indirectly controlled by Mr. Fu Wei, and thus the Seller is an associate of Mr. Fu Wei and
CBC Group. Accordingly, the Seller is a connected person of the Company and the
transactions contemplated under the Share Purchase Agreement constitutes a connected
transaction of the Company under Chapter 14A of the Listing Rules.

announcement, no other Shareholder has a material interest in or is otherwise interested in or
involved in the Share Purchase Agreement, and therefore, no other Shareholder is required
to abstain from voting on resolutions in relation to these matters at the EGM.
