Investment targets 
 selection criteria
The Fund intends to invest principally in mature-stage and growth￾stage projects, while allocating a relatively smaller portion of its 
capital to seed-stage projects.

5
For mature-stage projects, the Fund will generally prioritise 
established nuclear technology application enterprises with validated 
business models, stable management and technical teams, established 
customer bases and revenue streams, positive or near-positive cash 
flow, competitive positions in their respective market segments and 
identifiable exit prospects, including the potential for an IPO or 
industrial acquisition within approximately three to five years.
For growth-stage projects, the Fund will primarily focus on sectors 
with relatively clear growth prospects, including innovative 
radiopharmaceuticals, nuclear medical equipment and irradiation 
applications. Target companies would generally be expected to 
have products that have reached the clinical, pilot production or 
more advanced stages, or have otherwise achieved commercial 
validation; possess proprietary intellectual property or identifiable 
technological barriers; have management teams with both research 
and development capability and commercial experience; demonstrate 
a clear path to profitability; and possess differentiated competitive 
advantages and the potential to become leading participants in their 
respective market segments.
For seed-stage projects, the Fund will selectively invest in 
commercialisable technological achievements originating from 
national research institutes and research institutes affiliated 
with central state-owned enterprises, particularly technologies 
which may address strategically important technological 
bottlenecks in the nuclear technology application industry. Such 
investments are intended to provide long-term capital support 
for the commercialisation of original and potentially disruptive 
technologies.
Deployment plan The Fund has an investment period of four years from the first 
closing date. The partners are expected to contribute their respective 
capital commitments in two instalments of 40% and 60%. Other 
than the first instalment, CNNC Fund may generally initiate the 
subsequent capital call only after 80% of the preceding contribution 
has been utilised or reserved for investments approved by the 
investment committee, Fund expenses or other permitted purposes. 
Accordingly, capital will be deployed progressively by reference to 
the actual progress of suitable investments.
During the investment period, the Fund is expected to deploy 
capital substantially in accordance with the three-track allocation 
and to maintain a diversified portfolio across different stages of 
development, with mature-stage and growth-stage investments 
forming the principal part of the portfolio and seed-stage projects 
accounting for a relatively limited portion.

Distribution: (I) Cash distribution
1. Distribution of cash income received from investment

(3) The excess profit will be shared on a pro rata

with the relative ratio of the paid-in capital 
contribution amount by each limited partner.
2. Timing for distribution of cash income:
In principle, no later than 90 days after the Fund receives 
the relevant proceeds.

3. Upon expiry or termination and liquidation of the

III. REASONS AND BENEFITS FOR ENTERING INTO THE PARTNERSHIP 
AGREEMENT
The Fund has clearly defined investment scope and focuses on the nuclear technology 
application sector, including the production and preparation of isotopes, nuclear medical 
equipment and innovative drugs, industrial irradiation and inspection, security inspection 
and protection, as well as relevant applications of nuclear technology in the industrial, 
agricultural, medical, environmental protection and safety fields, together with key upstream 
and downstream links. The aforementioned investment directions are consistent with the 
Company’s existing business layout and its long-term strategic development direction. As 
a leading enterprise in the nuclear technology application field in China, the Company 
intends to leverage the investment deployment of the Fund across the niche segments of 
the nuclear technology application industry to create the synergistic effect of the “fund + 
industry” model. This will enable the Company to indirectly participate in the innovation 
and incubation of cutting-edge technologies, thereby reducing the cost of trial and error in 
independent research and development. Through the Fund, in addition to obtaining financial 
returns, the Company may secure preferential access to project cooperation rights, results 
commercialization rights and even strategic merger and acquisition opportunities, and rapidly 
transform external innovative achievements into new growth drivers for its own business. 
Leveraging the Fund’s industry events, project cooperation and other platforms, the Company 
may strengthen its exchanges and cooperation with peers and upstream and downstream 
enterprises along the industry chain, expand the scope for business cooperation, and empower 
its long-term development.
Pursuant to the currently applicable state-owned capital management system, if the Company 
makes direct investments, such investments would, in principle, be required to fall within 
the scope of the Company’s principal business. In addition, from the formation of an 
investment intention and project initiation through to the finalisation of the investment 
decision, the Company is required to undergo relatively complex internal decision-making 
and external approval procedures, with the overall process generally taking approximately 8 
to 12 months. Therefore, if the Company conducts investments primarily for the purpose of 
obtaining investment returns, the direct investment approach would entail relatively higher 
procedural and time costs. In contrast, through participation in the Fund, the Company’s 
investment capital is deployed by the Fund in a unified manner for project screening, 
investment decision-making and investment implementation, pursuant to the established 
investment strategy stipulated in the Partnership Agreement and under the decision-making 
and management mechanism of the meeting of the partners, the Investment Committee and 
the fund manager. Based on the past experience of similar funds, investments made through 
a fund generally take an average of approximately 3 to 4 months from project initiation to 
the completion of the investment decision. Accordingly, participation in the Fund helps to 
significantly reduce the procedural and time costs in the investment decision-making process 
and improve the efficiency of capital allocation and utilisation.

[PDF PAGE 11]
11
As to the risks in connection with the entering into the Partnership Agreement, the Company 
makes a relatively substantial investment in the Fund, with investment capital mainly to 
be recovered through exits from projects invested by the Fund; given the Fund’s four-year 
investment period and four-year exit period, with project exits concentrated in the latter part 
of the exit period, the Company’s investment may entail a lengthy capital recovery cycle, 
which may impair capital utilisation efficiency and give rise to the risk of long-term capital 
tie-up. In addition, the nuclear technology application sector features long research and 
development and commercialisation cycles, slow market cultivation and high susceptibility 
to policies and standards, and when compounded by technological iteration, cost volatility, 
insufficient capacity roll-out and mismatches between the Fund’s term and expected project 
exits, there exists a risk that investment returns may fall short of expectations.
In assessing the Partnership Agreement, the Directors have considered, among other things:
(a) the close alignment between the Fund’s exclusive focus on the nuclear technology 
application industry and the Group’s existing businesses and long-term development 
strategy;
(b) the potential financial returns and strategic benefits;
(c) the CNNC Fund’s accumulated extensive experience and project sourcing capabilities in 
the relevant industry;
(d) the benefits of obtaining diversified exposure to projects across different sub-sectors 
and stages of development through a professionally managed investment platform, as 
compared with the Group independently sourcing and managing a broad portfolio of 
minority investments;
(e) the investment restrictions, diversification measures, staged capital contribution 
mechanism, reporting arrangements and other investor protection mechanisms under the 
Partnership Agreement; and
(f) the risks associated with the investment.
The Directors have also considered the amount of the Company’s capital commitment 
relative to the Fund’s total committed capital, as well as the Group’s existing cash resources, 
liquidity position and anticipated capital expenditure requirements, and are of the view that 
the capital commitment will not have a material adverse impact on the Group’s operations 
or financial position. Having balanced the benefits and risks and made all reasonable 
enquiries, the Directors are of the view that the Company’s participation in the Fund 
represents a commercially reasonable means of obtaining financial and strategic exposure to 
investment opportunities in the nuclear technology application industry, and that the terms 
of the transaction are fair and reasonable and are in the interests of the Company and its 
shareholders as a whole.
