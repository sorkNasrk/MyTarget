The Fund’s investment strategy is to identify enterprises and
projects in the above sectors with technological
competitiveness, commercialisation potential and growth
prospects and make investments therein, thereby generating
investment returns.
Investment targets
selection criteria
The Fund intends to invest principally in mature-stage and
growth-stage projects, while allocating a relatively smaller
portion of its capital to seed-stage projects.
For mature-stage projects, the Fund will generally prioritise
established nuclear technology application enterprises with
validated business models, stable management and technical
teams, established customer bases and revenue streams,
positive or near-positive cash flow, competitive positions in
their respective market segments and identifiable exit
prospects, including the potential for an IPO or industrial
acquisition within approximately three to five years.
For growth-stage projects, the Fund will primarily focus on
sectors with relatively clear growth prospects, including
innovative radiopharmaceuticals, nuclear medical
equipment and irradiation applications. Target companies
would generally be expected to have products that have
reached the clinical, pilot production or more advanced
stages, or have otherwise achieved commercial validation;
possess proprietary intellectual property or identifiable
technological barriers; have management teams with both
research and development capability and commercial
experience; demonstrate a clear path to profitability; and
possess differentiated competitive advantages and the
potential to become leading participants in their respective
market segments.
For seed-stage projects, the Fund will selectively invest in
commercialisable technological achievements originating
from national research institutes and research institutes
affiliated with central state-owned enterprises, particularly
technologies which may address strategically important
technological bottlenecks in the nuclear technology
application industry. Such investments are intended to
provide long-term capital support for the commercialisation
of original and potentially disruptive technologies.

Deployment plan The Fund has an investment period of four years from the
first closing date. The partners are expected to contribute
their respective capital commitments in two instalments of
40% and 60%. Other than the first instalment, CNNC Fund
may generally initiate the subsequent capital call only after
80% of the preceding contribution has been utilised or
reserved for investments approved by the investment
committee, Fund expenses or other permitted purposes.
Accordingly, capital will be deployed progressively by
reference to the actual progress of suitable investments.
During the investment period, the Fund is expected to
deploy capital substantially in accordance with the three￾track allocation and to maintain a diversified portfolio
across different stages of development, with mature-stage
and growth-stage investments forming the principal part of
the portfolio and seed-stage projects accounting for a
relatively limited portion.

IV. Reasons and Benefits for Entering into the Partnership Agreement
The Fund has clearly defined investment scope and focuses on the nuclear technology
application sector, including the production and preparation of isotopes, nuclear medical
equipment and innovative drugs, industrial irradiation and inspection, security inspection and
protection, as well as relevant applications of nuclear technology in the industrial, agricultural,
medical, environmental protection and safety fields, together with key upstream and
downstream links. The aforementioned investment directions are consistent with the
Company’s existing business layout and its long-term strategic development direction. As a
leading enterprise in the nuclear technology application field in China, the Company intends
to leverage the investment deployment of the Fund across the niche segments of the nuclear
technology application industry to create the synergistic effect of the “fund + industry” model.
This will enable the Company to indirectly participate in the innovation and incubation of
cutting-edge technologies, thereby reducing the cost of trial and error in independent research
and development. Through the fund, in addition to obtaining financial returns, the Company
may secure preferential access to project cooperation rights, results commercialization rights
and even strategic merger and acquisition opportunities, and rapidly transform external
innovative achievements into new growth drivers for its own business. Leveraging the fund’s
industry events, project cooperation and other platforms, the Company may strengthen its
exchanges and cooperation with peers and upstream and downstream enterprises along the
industry chain, expand the scope for business cooperation, and empower its long-term
development.
Pursuant to the currently applicable state-owned capital management system, if the
Company makes direct investments, such investments would, in principle, be required to fall
within the scope of the Company’s principal business. In addition, from the formation of an
investment intention, project initiation to the finalisation of the investment decision, the
Company is required to undergo relatively complex internal decision-making and external
approval procedures, with the overall process generally taking approximately 8 to 12 months.
Therefore, if the Company conducts investments primarily for the purpose of obtaining
investment returns, the direct investment approach would entail relatively higher procedural
and time costs. In contrast, through participation in the Fund, the Company’s investment
capital is deployed by the Fund in a unified manner for project screening, investment
decision-making and investment implementation, which shall be conducted pursuant to the
LETTER FROM THE BOARD
– 16 –

[PDF PAGE 19]
established investment strategy stipulated in the Partnership Agreement and under the
decision-making and management mechanism of the meeting of all the partners of the Fund,
the Investment Committee and the fund manager. Based on the past experience of similar
funds, investments made through a fund generally take an average of approximately 3 to 4
months from project initiation to the completion of the investment decision. Accordingly,
participation in the Fund helps to significantly reduce the procedural and time costs in the
investment decision-making process and improve the efficiency of capital allocation and
utilisation of the Company.
As to the risks for entering into the Partnership Agreement, the Company makes a
relatively substantial investment in the Fund, with investment capital mainly to be recovered
through exits from projects invested by the Fund; given the Fund’s four-year investment period
and four-year exit period, with project exits concentrated in the latter part of the exit period,
the Company’s investment may entail a lengthy capital recovery cycle, which may impair
capital utilisation efficiency and give rise to the risk of long-term capital tie-up. In addition,
the nuclear technology application sector features long research-and-development and
commercialisation cycles, slow market cultivation and high susceptibility to policies and
standards, and when compounded by technological iteration, cost volatility, insufficient
capacity roll-out and mismatches between the Fund’s term and expected project exits, there
exists a risk that investment returns may fall short of expectations.
In assessing the Partnership Agreement, the Directors have considered, among other
things:
(a) the close alignment between the Fund’s exclusive focus on the nuclear technology
application industry and the Group’s existing businesses and long-term development
strategy;
(b) the potential financial returns and strategic benefits;
(c) the CNNC Fund’s established experience and project sourcing capabilities in the
relevant industry;
(d) the benefits of obtaining diversified exposure to projects across different sub-sectors
and stages of development through a professionally managed investment platform,
as compared with the Group independently sourcing and managing a broad portfolio
of minority investments;
(e) the investment restrictions, diversification measures, staged capital contribution
mechanism, reporting arrangements and other investor protection mechanisms under
the Partnership Agreement; and
(f) the risks associated with the investment.

preferential access to project cooperation rights, results commercialization rights and even

achievements into new growth drivers for its own business. Leveraging the fund’s industry
events, project cooperation and other platforms, the Company may strengthen its exchanges
and cooperation with peers and upstream and downstream enterprises along the industry chain,

Capital Contribution
Capital contributions under all Comparable Agreements are to be made in instalments
upon receipt of notice from the fund manager or the general partner. The specific proportion
for each contribution varies among the Comparable Agreements. We consider that the initial
capital contribution of 40% under the Fund Agreement is not materially higher than the initial
contribution proportions observed in the Comparable Agreements.
Duration
Among the seven Comparable Agreements, five have a total fund term of seven to eight
years, comprising an investment period of four to five years and an exit period of three to four
years. Among the Comparable Agreements, (i) two provide for either a single two-year
extension or two one-year extensions of the exit period; (ii) one provides for two one-year
extensions of the investment period; (iii) one allows one-year extension for both the investment
period and the exit period; and (iv) one provides for two one-year extensions applicable to
either the investment period or the exit period. The duration structure (including two one-year
extensions) under the Fund Agreement is generally in line with those of the Comparable
Agreements.
Management Fee
The management fees under the Comparable Agreements range from 1.0% to 2.2% per
annum during the investment period, and from 0.1% to 2.2% per annum during the exit period.
The management fee payable under the Fund Agreement falls within the range of the
Comparable Agreements.
Distribution
Among the seven Comparable Agreements, five adopt a standard distribution mechanism,
being: (i) return of capital contributions to each partner; (ii) payment of hurdle return to each
partner; and (iii) distribution of excess profits between the general partners and the limited
partners. The Fund Agreement’s distribution terms, comprising an 8% hurdle rate and an excess
profit distribution ratio of 20:80 between the general partners and the limited partners, are
generally consistent with the market practice reflected in the Comparable Agreements.
We noted that two of the Comparable Agreements stipulate that non-cash distributions
shall be subject to the approval of the partners’ meeting, without explicitly disclosing the
specific circumstances under which a non-cash distribution may be proposed. Given that a
non-cash distribution under the Fund Agreement will only be proposed if the relevant
investments cannot be realized or if such distribution would better serve the interests of all
partners, and that any non-cash distribution requires the unanimous consent of all partners, we
consider that providing flexibility for non-cash distributions is fair and reasonable. This
flexibility is particularly justified considering that investments in growth-stage or seed-stage
companies generally require a longer holding period.

Supervision
Under the Fund Agreement, the Company, as one of the limited partners, is entitled to
appoint one observer to the Investment Committee. Observer may attend Investment
Committee meetings without voting rights or participation in decision-making, but is entitled
to supervise compliance matters such as meeting notices, decision-making materials, and
investment procedures. Among the seven Comparable Agreements, three allow the limited
partner to appoint one member to the investment committee, while one allows the limited
partner to appoint one observer to the investment committee. Although the Company is not
entitled to appoint any member to the Investment Committee, the structure of appointing an
observer solely in its capacity as a limited partner for oversight purposes is not uncommon in
the market. We consider that the information, attendance, and inquiry rights granted to the
Company’s observer form a closed-loop supervision mechanism, which adequately enables the
Company to effectively monitor the fund’s investment operations and safeguards its capital
security and investment returns as a limited partner. In addition, the National Industrial
Investment Fund, as the largest limited partner and an independent third party, is entitled to
appoint one member to the Investment Committee. This member can participate in investment
decisions, thereby supervising the management and operation of the fund on behalf of other
limited partners who are not associated with CNNC.
